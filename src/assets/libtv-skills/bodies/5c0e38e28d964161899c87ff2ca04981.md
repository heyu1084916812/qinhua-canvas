# Write Commercial Shorts

Create original short films that enter quickly, clarify what the protagonist is doing, escalate through causal conflict pulses, deliver several local peaks, and end shortly after the final payoff.

## Start by identifying the task

Choose the smallest workflow that satisfies the request:

- **Vague seed or fragment**: Read `references/seed-to-story.md`.
- **Premise, synopsis, or incomplete outline**: Read `references/pacing-and-conflict-pulses.md`.
- **Full beat sheet or scene outline**: Read `references/screenplay-and-diagnosis.md`.
- **Existing screenplay**: Read `references/screenplay-and-diagnosis.md` and, when dialogue is in scope, `references/dialogue-and-visual-writing.md`.
- **Reference films, images, scripts, or style examples**: Read `references/reference-analysis-and-originality.md`.
- **Collaborative story development across multiple turns**: Read `references/structural-checkpoint.md` and use its checkpoint at the end of every development turn.

Read only the references required for the current task.

## Establish constraints

Extract or reasonably infer:

- target runtime
- genre and intended audience
- live action, animation, AI video, or other medium
- production limits such as cast, locations, effects, violence, and budget
- requested deliverable: directions, logline, synopsis, beats, scenes, screenplay, or diagnosis
- elements the user does not want changed

Ask no more than three focused questions at once. Prefer one decisive question during collaborative development. If the user requests a direct draft, state necessary assumptions and proceed.

## Preserve the creative core

Retain the user’s strongest image, relationship, rule, action, emotion, or ending. Do not replace a distinctive seed with a generic genre plot.

Separate:

- what the user explicitly established
- provisional solutions introduced to continue the discussion
- discarded directions

Never treat silence as approval. When the user corrects a premise, update the working interpretation immediately.

## Build a visible dramatic engine

Before expanding lore, establish:

- protagonist
- visible, judgeable goal
- why action is necessary now
- obstacle or opposing force
- failure cost
- repeatable mechanism that can generate escalating trouble

Express the central action as:

`protagonist + urgent visible goal + direct obstacle + consequential stakes`

Prefer verbs and decisions over biography or abstract theme.

## Use conflict pulses

Do not treat the film as setup followed by one final climax. Build 2–5 causal pulses appropriate to runtime:

`goal → action → obstacle → escalation → local climax → result → new situation`

Each result must force a new decision, strategy, relationship, or understanding. Connect events through “because” and “therefore,” not merely “and then.”

Make later pulses change at least one dimension:

- difficulty
- cost
- time pressure
- scope
- information
- relationship
- power
- morality
- identity
- world rule
- irreversibility

Read `references/pacing-and-conflict-pulses.md` for runtime models and final-payoff options.

## Design the final payoff separately

The final payoff is not required to be the loudest action scene. Choose the form best supported by the story:

- major confrontation
- decisive character choice
- hidden plan revealed
- identity reconstructed
- worldview or rule collapse
- causal loop closed
- planted information converged
- ironic success
- open external outcome after a completed internal choice

Make the payoff reinterpret, complete, or concentrate earlier material. Plant support before drafting. End within roughly 5–10% of runtime after the payoff unless a deliberate longer aftermath is essential.

## Control complexity

Add only information required for the current story action.

- Do not create an organization, technology history, mythology, or complete character biography to solve a local problem.
- Introduce at most one major new rule when one rule can drive the film.
- Show the phenomenon before explaining its cause.
- Prefer a simple causal link over an elaborate theory.
- If removing a setting detail does not damage action, escalation, choice, or payoff, remove it.
- If complexity increases without making the story clearer or more compelling, simplify.

## Write visually

Write only what can be seen or heard. Replace psychology with behavior, hesitation, physical choices, framing, sound, and consequences.

Let dialogue handle immediate intention, tactics, concealment, relationship, and pressure. Let images carry danger, world rules, atmosphere, and most exposition.

Read `references/dialogue-and-visual-writing.md` before a dialogue pass.

## Draft and diagnose

For a full screenplay:

1. Allocate a runtime budget before drafting.
2. Give each scene a visible dramatic action.
3. Mark estimated scene duration during development.
4. Write standard, shootable scene headings, action, and dialogue.
5. Audit cumulative runtime.
6. Diagnose structure, causality, escalation, payoff, visual clarity, dialogue, and production feasibility.

Use `references/screenplay-and-diagnosis.md` and copy the appropriate template from `assets/` when useful.

## Use references without copying

When users cite an existing film or series, extract high-level traits such as entry speed, information release, conflict rhythm, visual density, tonal contrast, or ending shape. Do not reuse protected characters, distinctive settings, dialogue, scene sequences, or a recognizable combination of plot elements.

For requests invoking the pacing or feeling of *Love, Death & Robots*, translate the request into:

- immediate entry into a charged situation
- rapid clarity of task or motivation
- visual rather than explanatory worldbuilding
- several causal escalations with local peaks
- a late decisive payoff or reinterpretation
- a short aftermath

Do not claim to reproduce a specific episode or creator’s exact style.

## End collaborative development turns with a checkpoint

When developing a story through conversation, end every turn with the compact structure-only checkpoint defined in `references/structural-checkpoint.md`.

Do not use the checkpoint when the user asks only for a finished artifact without ongoing development.
