# Chinese Celestial Palace Imagery

## Core Methodology

### Framework 1: Space Hierarchy Law — Architecture Dominates Humanity

The fundamental principle is **architecture-to-human ratio ≥ 8:1**. Every scene constructs a three-tier spatial compression system:

- **Tier 1 (Foreground Frame)**: Columns, archways, railings, or doorframes — create nested picture-in-picture composition at 10-20% of canvas
- **Tier 2 (Middle Ground Expanse)**: Cloud seas, corridors, plazas — occupy 50-60% of canvas, containing tiny human figures
- **Tier 3 (Background Monument)**: Main palace architecture or distant peaks — occupy 20-30% at horizon line

Human figure height MUST be 1/15 to 1/20 of canvas height. The environment is the protagonist; humans are visual anchors, not subjects.

**Spatial composition logic**:

- **Vertical dimension emphasis**: Use low-angle shots (looking up at towering stairs, monumental columns) or high-angle views (looking down at cloud-walking figures) to amplify sacred verticality
- **Horizontal plane extension**: Corridors, bridges, plazas all use one-point perspective converging to infinity
- **Floating/suspended elements**: Every scene should include at least one suspended structure (floating platforms, sky bridges, hanging waterfalls, cloud terraces) to establish "suspended between heaven and earth" ethereality

### Framework 2: Symmetry Axis + Frame Nesting

**Central axis symmetry** is the default for palace, corridor, and stairway scenes. Only use asymmetric composition for emotional moments (solitude/turning points).

**Frame nesting increases depth**: Utilize columns, circular moon gates, archways, dragon pillars as foreground frames to embed characters within frames, forming layered picture-in-picture structures.

**Three-character composition** is the fundamental relationship unit:

- Two characters: dialogue/intimacy
- Three characters: triangular composition (standard for group shots)
- Four characters: panoramic establishment

### Framework 3: Cold-Dominant Warm-Accent Color Formula

**Background/architecture**: Cold blue-gray with low saturation (moon white, ice blue, stone gray) **Character costumes**: 1-2 high-saturation warm colors as sole visual focus (red, emerald, gold) **Sky/cloud sea**: White mist diffusion, ultra-low saturation **Night scenes**: Deep blue night sky + cold moonlight

**Color temperature strategy**:

- Maintain overall environment at 60-70% desaturation
- Character costume saturation at 80-90%
- Use warm golden palace roofs as secondary accent (appears only in 20-30% of shots)

### Framework 4: Diffused Soft Light + Rim Light

**Ambient light**: Diffused light in cloud-filled scenes with no hard shadows, emphasizing "ethereal mist" **Top light/sacred light**: Light beams descending from skylights/heights in palace interiors, illuminating character crowns (religious sense) **Rim/edge light**: Characters appear as silhouettes or half-silhouettes in cliff and corridor scenes, with rim light outlining flowing garments **Cold moonlight**: Night scenes use blue-white cold light paired with dark architecture for solitude/mystery

**Light's function is not illumination, but atmosphere creation**. Light constructs layers of mood, not visibility.

## Workflow

### 1. Preparation Stage

**Scene type selection checklist**:

- \[ \] Architectural space (corridors, plazas, halls) — emphasize vertical/horizontal scale
- \[ \] Natural fusion (waterfalls, lone pines, cliff platforms) — architecture + landscape integration
- \[ \] Ceremonial scenes (altars, grand halls) — maximize sacred sense
- \[ \] Dynamic narrative (crane guidance, walking sequences) — suitable for video storyboards

**Character configuration**:

- \[ \] 1 person = solitude/contemplation mood
- \[ \] 2 people = dialogue/intimacy
- \[ \] 3 people = standard group shot (triangular composition)
- \[ \] 4+ people = crowd scale/ceremony sense

**Time of day decision**:

- \[ \] Daytime: Cold blue-white base, golden palace accent
- \[ \] Dusk: Warm orange clouds vs cool architecture
- \[ \] Night: Deep blue sky + cold moonlight + minimal warm light points

### 3. Iteration Stage

**Dimension 1: Spatial Proportion Audit**

- \[ \] Is human figure height ≤ 1/15 of canvas? (If too large, add "extremely tiny distant figures" constraint)
- \[ \] Does architecture occupy 70%+ of canvas? (If insufficient, expand background palace or add foreground columns)
- \[ \] Are there 3+ spatial layers visible? (Foreground frame → middle cloud sea → background palace)
- \[ \] Does the main structure clearly extend to vanishing point?

**Dimension 2: Composition Axis Check**

- \[ \] For symmetrical scenes (corridors/altars/plazas): Is the central axis precisely vertical?
- \[ \] For frame nesting: Do foreground columns/archways clearly enclose middle-ground characters?
- \[ \] For three-character shots: Do figures form triangular relationship positions?

**Dimension 3: Color Temperature Audit**

- \[ \] Is environment saturation ≤ 70%? (If too saturated, specify "low saturation cool blue-gray")
- \[ \] Are character costume colors the sole warm accent? (Red, green, gold)
- \[ \] Does color temperature contrast meet 60:40 cold-to-warm ratio?

**Dimension 4: Light Quality Check**

- \[ \] Is ambient light diffused soft light without hard shadows?
- \[ \] Do characters have rim/edge light outlining silhouettes?
- \[ \] For palace interiors: Do sacred light beams descend from above?

**When to regenerate vs. accept**:

- Regenerate: Human figures too large, spatial proportions chaotic, color temperature imbalanced
- Accept: Minor architectural detail variations, slight cloud sea differences — these add natural diversity

## Key Constraints & Standards

### Immutable Rules (Zero Tolerance)

1. **Model constraint**: MUST use Midjourney Style Image V8.2 (\[@tool:mj-v8.2:Style Image V8.2\]). Prohibit referencing specific historical images; derive style from text descriptions only.
2. **Human proportion rule**: Character height = 1/15 to 1/20 of canvas height (approximately 5-7% of total canvas area)
3. **Architecture dominance rule**: Architecture and cloud sea MUST occupy 80%+ of canvas; humans occupy ≤ 5%
4. **Perspective unity rule**: Within a single frame, maintain consistent perspective (do not mix overhead view foreground with low-angle background)
5. **Suspended element requirement**: Every scene MUST include at least one floating/suspended structure (sky bridge, cliff platform, floating island, cloud terrace)

### Fidelity Rules (Quality Standards)

 6. **Spatial layer requirement**: Minimum 4 visible layers — foreground frame (10-20%) → middle cloud sea (40-50%) → character zone (5%) → background palace (20-30%)
 7. **Central axis symmetry**: Palace, corridor, altar, plaza scenes default to strict central axis symmetry; asymmetry only for emotional shots
 8. **Frame nesting frequency**: 60%+ of shots should utilize foreground columns/archways/gates as framing elements
 9. **Vanishing point extension**: Corridors, bridges, stairs MUST visibly extend to vanishing point (not cut off mid-frame)
10. **Cold-to-warm ratio**: Color temperature distribution — cold tones 70%, warm tones 30% (warm tones concentrated in character costumes + minimal palace roof accents)

### Consistency Rules (Cross-Frame Coherence)

11. **Character costume color binding**: Core characters bind to fixed costume colors (red/green/blue/white), maintained consistently across all shots as visual identification anchors
12. **Architectural style unity**: Palace roof style (curved eaves/tiered roofs), column type (jade/vermillion/carved), material texture (jade/gilded/wooden) — maintain consistency within same scene sequence
13. **Cloud sea density standard**: Within same time period, cloud sea density and altitude should remain relatively consistent; density changes only between different time-of-day sequences
14. **Light direction coherence**: Within same scene, sacred light/rim light direction must remain consistent; backlight requires justification (sunset position, moon angle)

## Advanced Techniques

### Technique 1: Asymmetric Breaking for Emotional Emphasis

While central axis symmetry is default, **intentional asymmetric composition** creates emotional tension:

- Single character departing from central axis (loneliness/exile)
- Off-center vanishing point (unease/turning point)
- Left-right imbalanced foreground frames (narrative directionality)

Use frequency: ≤ 20% of total shots, reserved for climactic emotional moments.

### Technique 2: Time-of-Day Color Temperature Progression

Create narrative rhythm through systematic color temperature shifts:

- **Dawn/morning**: Cool blue-white + minimal warm orange clouds (pure, hopeful)
- **Midday**: Balanced cool-warm, highest visibility (exploration, activity)
- **Dusk**: Warm orange-gold clouds vs. cool architecture (farewell, contemplation)
- **Night**: Deep blue + cold moonlight + sparse warm light points (mystery, solitude)

Within a story sequence, arrange time-of-day progression to guide emotional arc.

### Technique 3: Character Count as Emotional Punctuation

Character quantity directly controls emotional density:

- 1 person = introspection/solitude (slow pacing)
- 2 people = dialogue/intimacy (medium pacing)
- 3 people = standard group dynamic (balanced pacing)
- 4+ people = ceremony/crowd (grand pacing)

In narrative sequences, use character count variation to create rhythmic breathing: Example: Solo shot → three-person group → solo shot → two-person dialogue → four-person ceremony

### Technique 4: Foreground Frame Depth Stacking

For maximum depth, stack multiple frame layers:

- Layer 1: Nearest columns (10% of frame, semi-transparent silhouette)
- Layer 2: Middle archway (30% of frame, semi-clear structure)
- Layer 3: Distant doorway (50% of frame, clear detail)
- Layer 4: Far palace (horizon line, hazy outline)

This creates **4-layer nested picture-in-picture**, achieving ultimate spatial depth. Use sparingly (≤ 10% of shots) to avoid visual fatigue.
