# Micro-Documentary Filmmaking

## Core Methodology

### Character-Centered Narrative Architecture

Micro-documentaries derive their emotional power from intimate character portraiture. The methodology follows a three-layer construction:

**Layer 1 — Protagonist Identity Foundation**: Establish the character through their professional identity and daily environment. For a taxi driver, this means the vehicle interior as primary setting, the rhythm of fares and routes, the physical gestures of driving. The occupation is not background—it is the lens through which the theme emerges.

**Layer 2 — Thematic Thread Weaving**: Identify a universal human value that the character's specific experiences illustrate. "Small trust builds great trust" (小信成则大信立) works because it translates abstract Confucian ethics into concrete actions: returning lost items, maintaining vehicle cleanliness, consistent courtesy across all passengers. The theme must be discovered through specifics, not imposed as narration.

**Layer 3 — Verisimilitude Through Restriction**: Realism emerges from constraint. Limit yourself to:

- Diegetic sound only (no score overlays)
- Natural lighting within actual locations
- Handheld or vehicle-mounted camera positions
- Dialogue that serves function, not exposition

This creates observational authenticity where the audience feels they are witnessing, not being told.

### Temporal Compression Framework

For 3-5 minute micro-docs, time must be compressed without losing narrative breath:

**Micro-Beat Structure** (not traditional three-act):

1. **Establishment** (30-45 seconds): Location + character + routine
2. **Inciting Detail** (45-60 seconds): The specific incident that reveals character (finding the lost item)
3. **Choice Point** (60-90 seconds): The protagonist's decision-making process shown through action
4. **Resonance** (30-45 seconds): The consequence that proves the theme
5. **Return to Rhythm** (15-30 seconds): Character returns to routine, but we see them differently now

Each beat must advance both plot and theme simultaneously. No beat exists solely for exposition.

### Visual Language of Intimacy

Micro-documentaries demand close observation. The cinematographic approach:

**Spatial Zones**:

- **Zone 1 (Intimate)**: Inside the vehicle—rearview mirror reflections, hands on steering wheel, meter clicks, passenger door handles. These shots establish the protagonist's workspace as both literal and psychological territory.
- **Zone 2 (Transitional)**: Through windows—cityscapes passing, weather changes, traffic patterns. These provide narrative ellipsis and passage of time without cutting away from the character's experience.
- **Zone 3 (Contextual)**: Exterior establishing shots—the taxi in the urban grid, waiting at stands, navigating intersections. Minimal but essential for geographic grounding.

Camera movement should mirror the protagonist's attention: static during waiting, mobile during driving, focused during interpersonal exchanges.

## Workflow

### 1. Pre-Production Phase

**Character Research & Access**:

- Conduct 2-3 hour ride-along sessions before filming to understand daily rhythm
- Identify 3-5 recurring situations that reveal character values (how they greet passengers, handle payment, maintain vehicle)
- Obtain authentic props from the character's actual work environment (taxi license, personal items, worn equipment)

**Thematic Anchor Selection**:

- Interview the character to discover their personal philosophy expressed in their own words
- Translate their language into a universal ethical principle
- Test the theme: Can it be shown through 3+ different specific actions without narration?

**Location Scouting Within Constraints**:

- Map the protagonist's actual routes and identify 2-3 visually distinctive locations along them
- Note natural light conditions at different times of day
- Identify ambient sound signatures (market areas, quiet residential streets, busy intersections)

**Shot List Development**: Create a modular shot list organized by setup type, not chronological sequence:

- **Interior vehicle shots** (protagonist alone, with passengers, detail shots of vehicle elements)
- **Point-of-view shots** (through windshield, rearview mirror perspectives)
- **Exterior vehicle shots** (approaching, departing, waiting)
- **Insert details** (hands, objects, meters, mirrors)

This allows efficient shooting across multiple days while maintaining narrative flexibility in editing.

### 2. Production Stage

**Capture Approach — Observational Priority**:

- Begin each shooting block with 10-15 minutes of rolling without direction to capture authentic behavior
- Use long takes (2-3 minutes minimum) to let moments develop naturally
- Shoot coverage at a 5:1 ratio minimum—micro-docs require extensive material to find genuine moments

**Audio Capture Strategy**:

- Lav mic on protagonist at all times
- Shotgun mic for ambient environment
- Record wild sound separately: engine hum, traffic ambience, radio chatter, meter beeps
- Capture silence—the protagonist alone between fares is narratively essential

**Directing Non-Actors**:

- Never ask the subject to "act" or "perform"
- Instead, ask them to "show me how you normally do \[task\]"
- If a moment feels inauthentic, stop and reset rather than continuing
- Use multiple takes only for technical issues, not performance refinement

**Scene Reconstruction Ethics**: If recreating a past event (like finding a lost item):

- Use the actual item or an identical substitute
- Have the protagonist re-perform their actual actions in the actual location
- Disclose in credits if a scene is reconstructed
- Never fabricate events that didn't occur

### 3. Post-Production Stage

**Assembly Edit — Finding the Story**:

- Ingest all footage and create a "selects" timeline of any moment with authentic emotional resonance (target 15-20 minutes from 1-2 hours of footage)
- Watch selects without sound to identify visual storytelling
- Watch selects without picture to identify audio storytelling
- Assemble a rough structure focusing on the single strongest through-line

**Narrative Refinement**:

- Apply the Micro-Beat Structure: mark where each beat currently falls and adjust pacing
- Remove any moment that doesn't advance both character and theme
- Test for "observational purity": does this feel witnessed or constructed?
- Cut on action and environmental sound, not on visual beauty

**Sound Design for Realism**:

- Build ambient bed from wild sound, not library effects
- Maintain diegetic logic—we only hear what the protagonist hears
- Use silence as a narrative tool—the absence of city noise when protagonist is alone with a decision
- Mix for intimacy: voice and close sounds slightly elevated, distant traffic and environment creating texture

**Color Grading Constraints**:

- Grade to match natural light conditions of the time of day shot
- Slight desaturation (5-10%) to signal documentary rather than commercial aesthetic
- Preserve skin tone accuracy—avoid stylistic color shifts that break verisimilitude
- Maintain shadow detail in vehicle interiors

**Title and Credit Design**:

- Use simple sans-serif typography in the language of the subject
- Position text in areas of low visual activity
- Keep titles on screen long enough to read twice at natural reading speed
- Credit real names and locations to reinforce documentary contract with audience

### 4. Review and Refinement

**Audience Testing Protocol**:

- Screen for 3-5 viewers unfamiliar with the project
- Ask only: "What is this person's story?" and "What do you think this is about?"
- If answers diverge significantly from intended theme, return to editing—the fault is in the cut, not the audience
- Watch for moments where viewers disengage (check phones, shift position)—these indicate pacing issues

**Final Quality Checks**:

- **Narrative clarity**: Can a viewer unfamiliar with the context understand the story?
- **Thematic consistency**: Does every scene contribute to the central ethical idea?
- **Authentic tone**: Does this feel observed or constructed? (Aim for observed)
- **Emotional arc**: Does the ending feel earned through the character's actions?
- **Technical invisibility**: Are there any shots/edits that draw attention to the filmmaking rather than the subject?

## Key Constraints & Standards

### Immutable Rules

1. **No external narration**: The protagonist's voice or observational silence only—never an omniscient narrator explaining the theme
2. **No non-diegetic music**: All sound must originate from the character's environment (radio in the taxi is acceptable, score is not)
3. **Actual locations only**: No studio recreations or controlled environments that didn't exist in the character's real experience
4. **Protagonist-present framing**: The character or their direct point of view must be present in 90%+ of shots—this is their story, not a topic documentary

### Fidelity Rules

1. **Temporal honesty**: If compressing time (e.g., showing events from multiple days as if continuous), use visual transitions that signal ellipsis—never create false continuity
2. **Dialogue authenticity**: If re-recording unclear audio, the character must speak their own actual words, not scripted improvements
3. **Prop accuracy**: Any object featured should be the character's actual possession or an identical substitute—avoid "prop department" aesthetics
4. **Environmental sound accuracy**: The soundscape must match the visual location—no mismatched ambience

### Consistency Rules

1. **Camera aesthetic**: Choose handheld OR locked-off tripod as primary approach and maintain 80%+ consistency—mixing styles signals different production values
2. **Color palette**: Maintain consistent time-of-day color temperature within scenes—don't mix golden hour and midday in the same sequence
3. **Depth of field**: Stay within one f-stop range across the project—micro-docs use narrow DOF for intimacy or deep focus for environment, but not both arbitrarily
4. **Edit rhythm**: Establish an average shot length in the first minute and stay within 30% variance—micro-docs live or die on pacing consistency

### Production Standards

1. **Shooting ratio**: Minimum 5:1 footage-to-final ratio for capturing authentic behavior
2. **Duration target**: 3-5 minutes final runtime—shorter loses thematic depth, longer loses micro-doc intimacy
3. **Location diversity**: 2-3 distinct locations maximum—more fragments focus, fewer limits visual range
4. **Character screen time**: Protagonist visible or voice present in 85%+ of runtime—this ensures character-driven structure

### Ethical Standards

1. **Informed consent**: Obtain written release from protagonist and any identifiable secondary subjects
2. **Reconstruction disclosure**: If recreating past events, include "Some scenes recreated" or equivalent in end credits
3. **Context preservation**: Edit for truth—never reassemble dialogue or actions to create meanings the subject didn't intend
4. **Dignity maintenance**: Avoid any edit choices that mock, condescend to, or exploit the subject's vulnerability
